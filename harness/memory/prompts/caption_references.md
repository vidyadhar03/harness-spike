## Task: caption candidate reference images
You are given the memory context for one location, then a numbered set of candidate images retrieved from an archive. Each image comes with its index, title, and description, followed by the image itself.

These are candidates for a director's reference board. Caption the ones worth keeping and leave out the rest.

- captions: one entry per image you keep. Do not caption an image you are dropping.
  - index: the image's index, exactly as given.
  - caption: 1 to 3 sentences describing what is actually visible: place type, layout, materials, condition, light, time of day, and scale cues. Describe the image, not the script. Never state that this image shows the production's location; it is a reference for how the place could look.
  - facet: what this image is a reference FOR.
    - place: a settlement or site that could stand in for the location itself. Same region, same kind of place.
    - architecture: buildings or construction that show the right technique or form, even if the setting is wrong.
    - material: surfaces, textures, walls, roofs, stonework. The frame may be from anywhere; what matters is the material.
    - terrain: landform, slope, vegetation, water, weather. The buildings in frame may be wrong.
    An image from another country with the right stone and roof is a good material reference and a bad place reference. Choose by what the image actually earns, not by how much of it matches.
- Drop images that are maps, diagrams, logos, portraits, close-ups of objects with no sense of place, or that show nothing relevant to the context. To drop an image, simply leave it out.
- Also drop images that contradict the context: a different part of the world where the region is named, modern infrastructure where the production has none, the wrong period, the wrong climate or vegetation. A well-photographed place that the production could never use is still a drop. If you find yourself writing a caption that describes something the context rules out, that is the signal to leave the image out entirely.
