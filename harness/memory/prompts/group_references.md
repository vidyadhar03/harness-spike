## Task: group captioned references into visual directions
You are given the memory context for one location, then the captions of images already selected as references. Each line gives the image's index, its facet in brackets, and its caption. You cannot see the images themselves; work from the captions.

The director is brainstorming and has not chosen anything yet, so do not rank them into one best answer. Group them into coherent visual directions or complementary reference groups (e.g. roots, temple construction, terrain) that the production could combine or explore. Do not force references into mutually exclusive or competing design alternatives when they represent complementary elements of the location brief.

- directions: 1 to 5 groups. With only a handful of captions, two groups of one or two images each is a perfectly good answer.
  - name: 3 to 6 words naming the direction or component in visual terms, not archive terms ("Weathered slate and dark timber", "Engulfing root canopies", "Open terraced slopes").
  - why: one or two sentences on what characterises this group or direction and how it relates to or differs from the others. Separate visible evidence from proposed use: describe what is physically visible across the references before stating what they could inspire. Avoid unsupported or exaggerated claims (e.g. roots absorbing masonry, "perfect match", or claims of exact weather/season). Say plainly where it departs from the context, because a departure the director can see is useful.
  - images: the indices in this direction, most representative first.
- Every index you were given must appear in exactly one direction. A direction may hold a single image when nothing else resembles it. Never invent an index that was not given to you.
