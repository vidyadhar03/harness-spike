## Task: group reference images into visual directions
You are given the memory context for one location, then a numbered set of candidate images retrieved from an archive. Each image comes with its index, title, and description, followed by the image itself.

The director is brainstorming and has not chosen anything yet, so do not rank the images into one best answer. Group them into distinct visual directions the production could take.

Work in this order: decide which images to keep, caption each one, then group the captioned images into directions. Never name an index in a direction that you have not captioned.

- captions: one entry per image you keep. Do not caption an image you are dropping.
  - index: the image's index.
  - caption: 1 to 3 sentences describing what is actually visible: place type, layout, materials, condition, light, time of day, and scale cues. Describe the image, not the script. Never state that this image shows the production's location; it is a reference for how the place could look.
  - facet: what this image is a reference FOR.
    - place: a settlement or site that could stand in for the location itself. Same region, same kind of place.
    - architecture: buildings or construction that show the right technique or form, even if the setting is wrong.
    - material: surfaces, textures, walls, roofs, stonework. The frame may be from anywhere; what matters is the material.
    - terrain: landform, slope, vegetation, water, weather. The buildings in frame may be wrong.
    An image from another country with the right stone and roof is a good material reference and a bad place reference. Choose by what the image actually earns, not by how much of it matches.
- directions: 1 to 5 groups. With only a handful of candidates, two groups of one or two images each is a perfectly good answer.
  - name: 3 to 6 words naming the direction in visual terms, not archive terms ("Weathered slate and dark timber", "Open terraced slopes").
  - why: one or two sentences on what characterises this direction and how it differs from the others. Say plainly where it departs from the context, because a departure the director can see is useful.
  - images: the indices in this direction, most representative first. An image belongs to exactly one direction.
- Drop images that are maps, diagrams, logos, portraits, close-ups of objects with no sense of place, or that show nothing relevant to the context. To drop an image, leave it out of both captions and directions.
- A direction may hold a single image when nothing else resembles it. Every image you caption must appear in exactly one direction.
